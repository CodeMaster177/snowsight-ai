\section{Problem Statement}

\subsection{Problem}

During winter season, municipalities are responsible for maintaining safe and
usable transportation infrastructure. Winter maintenance activities, including
snow clearing, plowing, and salting, are performed throughout large municipal
road networks. Such services may be performed directly by municipalities or by
independent contractors working on their behalf. Once maintenance has been
completed, municipalities need to verify whether the final road condition
satisfies the winter maintenance standard for that specific region.

The current records of maintenance activity do not clearly demonstrate the
quality or completeness of the work performed. For instance, a record showing
that a maintenance vehicle travelled along a road establishes that the vehicle
was present, but does not prove that the road was left in an acceptable
condition. Snow, ice, or debris conditions may still remain even after a
maintenance pass. Therefore, municipalities need concrete evidence of the
actual post-maintenance road condition instead of simply relying on records
indicating that maintenance activity has occurred.

Verification is further complicated as acceptable road conditions are not
always identical across municipalities. Different jurisdictions may
define different levels of service, priorities, or acceptable post-maintenance
requirements. A road state that is considered acceptable under one municipality's
standards may be considered unacceptable under another's. Evaluating
completed maintenance work requires both an assessment of the resulting road
condition and consideration of the standard applicable to the region in
which the work was performed.

The sheer scale of municipal road networks makes consistent verification challenging.
Manually examining large quantities of post-maintenance evidence requires
significant time and effort and may result in conflicting assessments between
different reviewers or road segments. Without a consistent assessment process,
municipalities may have difficulty identifying inadequate maintenance,
determining whether extra treatment is required, and evaluating whether a
contractor has fulfilled the expected standard level of service.

Reliable verification is also essential for contractor accountability and
payment decisions. Municipalities require sufficient evidence that contracted
work has achieved the expected standard before determining whether the work
qualifies for payment. Additioanlly, contractors have an interest in assessments
being consistent, traceable, and based on the standards under which their work
was performed.

The central problem is therefore the need for an objective, consistent, and
scalable means of assessing the condition of municipal roads after winter
maintenance, comparing that condition with the applicable municipal standard,
and providing sufficient evidence to support maintenance-quality and contractor
payment verification. The problem concerns assessing what condition the road
was left in and whether that condition is acceptable; the particular technical
method used to perform that assessment is outside the scope of the problem
definition.

\subsection{Inputs and Outputs}

At a high level overview, the problem has three primary inputs.

The first input is evidence representing the observed condition of a road
segment after winter maintenance services have occurred. This evidence
represents the physical state in which the road was left and may contain
observable conditions, like remaining snow, ice, slush, water, or other forms
of debris.

The second input is the location information linked with the observed road
segment. Location establishes which jurisdiction and road segment are being
assessed and allows the appropriate maintenance expectations to be associated
with the observation.

The third input is the related winter maintenance standard or
level-of-service criterion for that location. This defines the conditions
against which the observed road state is evaluated.

The primary output is a classification or assessment detailing the observed
post-maintenance road conditions. The evaluation must provide a consistent and
easily understandable representation of the road's condition.

A second output is a conclusion of whether the assessed road condition
satisfies the applicable maintenance standard. This output seperates work
that meets the relevant standard from work that fails to meet the standard or
requires additional review.

A third output is a verification result that can assist in contractor-payment
decisions. The result shows whether the available assessment supports the
conclusion that the maintenance work satisfied the applicable level of service or
whether the work should be referred for extra review. The final authorization
and transfer of payment remain governed by municipal policies and contractual
requirements and are not included in the core problem.

\subsection{Stakeholders}

\subsubsection{Municipalities}

Municipalities responsible for winter road maintenance are the primary
stakeholders. Municipal staff need to verify whether roads have been
maintained according to the standards set for their jurisdiction.
They require clear and reliable information about post-maintenance conditions
to assess service quality, identify work that may require additional
attention, and support operational and contractual decisions.

\subsubsection{Winter Maintenance Contractors}

Winter maintenance contractors are in charge of performing services such as
plowing, clearing, salting, and other treatments according to municipal and
contractual expectations. Since assessments of completed work may impact
performance evaluation and payment verification, contractors have an interest
in the assessment process being consistent, transparent, traceable, and based
on the applicable municipal standard.

\subsubsection{Municipal VU Consulting Inc.}

Municipal VU Consulting Inc. is the supervising organization and a project
stakeholder. The organization contributes domain knowledge related to
municipal winter maintenance operations, service standards, and maintenance
quality verification. Its involvement helps ensure that the problem and
resulting project goals reflect practical municipal needs.

\subsubsection{Municipal Operations and Contract Administration Staff}

Municipal staff responsible for winter operations, service monitoring, contract
administration, and contractor management are expected to use the resulting
assessment information. These users need to review road conditions, determine
whether certain standards have been satisfied, investigate unsatisfactory
results, and use the available evidence to support decisions.

\subsubsection{Road Users}

Drivers and other users of municipal road infrastructure are indirect
stakeholders. They rely on municipalities and maintenance providers to keep
transportation infrastructure reasonably safe and usable during winter
conditions. Even though road users are not expected to interact directly with the
system, improvements in the consistency of winter-maintenance verification can
support the quality of the services on which they depend.

\subsection{Environment}

\subsubsection{Physical Environment}

The problem exists within municipal winter road networks during winter
maintenance operations. Roads may be observed after plowing, salting, clearing,
or other maintenance activities. Conditions can vary significantly between road
segments and over time.

Post-maintenance road surfaces may contain certain amounts of snow, ice, slush,
water, exposed pavement, accumulated winter debris, or combinations of these
conditions. The surrounding environment may also contain vehicles, road
markings, intersections, parked vehicles, snowbanks, and other infrastructure.

Environmental conditions may include active snowfall, precipitation, overcast
weather, bright sunlight, darkness, reduced visibility, and changing
illumination. These variations are part of the operating environment and make
the appearance of otherwise similar road conditions non-uniform.

\subsubsection{User Hardware Environment}

Municipal staff are expected to review assessment results using regular
desktop or laptop computers. Users are not required to have specialized processing
hardware to review road-condition assessments, compliance results, or
verification records.

\subsubsection{User Software Environment}

The user-facing information is expected to be accessible through commonly
available modern web-browser software. Users should be able to review relevant
road observations, assessment results, applicable standards, and verification
information without interacting directly with the underlying processing
components.

The development tools, programming languages, processing algorithms, and
developer hardware used to implement the system are intentionally excluded
from this section because they do not define the users' operating environment.

\subsection{Scope}

The core scope of the project is the verification of winter maintenance quality
for municipal roadways after a maintenance or treatment activity has occurred.
The project focuses on assessing the resulting road state, determining whether
that state satisfies the standard applicable to its location, and producing
information that can support verification of contractor work.

The project does not replace municipal authorities in establishing winter
maintenance standards. Instead, existing standards are treated as inputs to the
assessment process. The project also does not make contractual policy or legal
decisions on behalf of municipalities.

Contractor payment verification is within the project scope, but the actual
transfer of funds is not part of the core system. The core system provides
evidence and a verification result that can support a payment decision. An
automated payment platform is treated as a stretch goal.

The core project focuses on roadways. Verification of sidewalks and bike lanes
is treated as a stretch goal so that the primary project remains focused and
achievable within the Capstone development period.

\section{Goals}

\subsection{G1: Recognition of Post-Maintenance Road State}

The system shall provide a consistent assessment of the observable condition of
a road segment once winter maintenance has occurred.

The assessment shall highlight the differences between the road-state categories established
for the project so that a post-maintenance observation can be assigned an
interpretable road-state label. For this goal, success will be evaluated using a
labelled set of representative road observations and an agreed-upon
classification performance measure.

This goal provides municipalities with an objective description of the
condition in which a road was left in instead of just confirming that a
maintenance vehicle travelled along the road.

\subsection{G2: Location-Specific Maintenance Assessment}

The system shall verify whether an assessed road condition meets the
winter maintenance standard applicable to the road's region.

Given a road-state assessment, its location, and the applicable municipal
standard, the system shall generate a clear compliance result indicating whether
the observed condition meets the relevant standard or requires additional
review.
Success for this goal will be measured by matching generated compliance
results against expected results for a set of predefined road-state and
municipal-standard scenarios.

This goal ensures that maintenance quality is evaluated according to the
requirements of the responsible municipality instead of applying a single
standard to each road.

\subsection{G3: Maintenance Work Verification}

The system shall output a traceable verification result for an assessed road
segment that associates the road condition with the specific location,
maintenance standard, and compliance result.

A municipal reviewer shall be able to determine from the verification result
why the maintenance work was identified as meeting the applicable standard,
not meeting the standard, or requiring further review. Success for this goal
will be evaluated by conlcuding that verification records have the
information necessary to trace the assessment to its associated observation,
location, standard level, and compliance result.

This goal provides municipalities and contractors with reviewable evidence of
how a maintenance-quality determination was attained.

\subsection{G4: Contractor Payment Verification}

The system shall use the maintenance compliance result to strengthen verification
of whether completed contractor work satisfies the maintenance conditions
associated with payment.

For an evaluated maintenance activity, the system shall provide a clear payment
verification status indicating that the work is supported for payment, is not
supported based on the applicable maintenance standard, or requires manual
review. Success for this goal will be assessed using predefined maintenance
and payment-verification scenarios with known predictable outcomes.

This goal is limited to aiding the payment-verification decision. Final
payment authorization, contractual interpretation, and transfer of funds remain
the responsibility of the municipality.

\subsection{G5: Usable Review of Verification Results}

The system shall present assessment and verification information in a form that
municipal users can comprehend and review without requiring knowledge of the
underlying processing methods involved.

A reviewer shall be able to identify the road segment, observed road state,
applicable standard, compliance result, and payment-verification status for an
assessment. Success for this goal will be assessed through representative
review tasks where users must locate and interpret the information required
to understand a verification result.

This goal ensures that the project's assessment capabilities produce useful
information for the municipal stakeholders who ultimately need to review and
act on the outcomes.

\section{Stretch Goals}

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Contractor Payment Platform:}
    Extend payment verification into payment execution by permitting an approved
    contractor payment to be initiated once the required municipal approval
    process has been finalized. This extends the core system beyond supporting
    payment-related decisions to facilitating the resulting transaction.

    \item \textbf{Dispute and Appeal Workflow:}
    Provide a structured process where contractors can dispute a
    maintenance assessment or payment-verification result. Municipal reviewers
    would be able to view the associated evidence, record a decision, and
    maintain a traceable history of the dispute.

    \item \textbf{Extended Verification for Sidewalk Lanes:}
    Extend maintenance-quality verification beyond only roadways to sidewalks and
    bicycle lanes. The extension would represent the different
    maintenance standards and observable conditions associated with these types
    of infrastructure.

    \item \textbf{Road Clearance Duration Estimation:}
    Estimate the duration for which an adequately treated road segment is expected to remain
    in an acceptable state by considering relevant environmental and treatment
    information. This could help municipalities in anticipating when additional
    maintenance may be required.

\end{itemize}

\section{Custom Documents (CDs)}

The Fall custom document will be the \textbf{Machine Learning (ML) Model
Report}. This document will describe and evaluate the model used to support
road-state recognition, including the data used for evaluation, the selected
evaluation measures, experimental results, limitations, and relevant design
decisions.

The Winter custom document will be the \textbf{Performance Report}. This
document will evaluate the completed system against relevant performance
objectives. The report will include quantitative measurements of system
performance and discuss whether the implemented system provides adequate
performance for its intended municipal use.

These custom documents may be refined with the approval of the course
instructor as the project progresses.
